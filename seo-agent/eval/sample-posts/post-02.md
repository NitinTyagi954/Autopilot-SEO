# The Dashboard Mistake Most Early-Stage Teams Make

When a team builds their first internal dashboard, the instinct is to
put everything on one screen — every metric anyone might ever want, all
at once. It feels thorough. It's usually a mistake.

The problem isn't that the numbers are wrong. It's that a dashboard
with forty tiles doesn't answer any question faster than a spreadsheet
would. Nobody opens it every day because nothing on it says "look here
first."

A better approach is to design around one decision at a time. Ask what
someone needs to decide this week, then build the smallest possible
view that answers exactly that. Everything else can live one click
away, not on the front screen.

![Screenshot comparing a cluttered dashboard to a focused one](comparison.png)

This is also where real-time data actually earns its keep — not by
showing more, but by making the one number that matters update the
moment it changes, instead of waiting for a nightly refresh.
