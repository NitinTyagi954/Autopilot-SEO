# Why Weekly Cohort Tracking Beats Monthly Reports

Most teams check their numbers once a month and call it good enough. But
by the time a monthly report lands, the trend that mattered already
happened three weeks ago.

Weekly cohort tracking flips that. Instead of one big snapshot, you get
a rolling view of how each group of users behaves over time — from
their first week onward. This makes it much easier to catch a drop in
engagement before it shows up as a churn number at the end of the
quarter.

![A line chart showing weekly cohort retention curves](chart.png)

Teams that already track weekly KPIs tend to adapt fastest, because
they're used to looking at short time windows instead of waiting for
month-end. Pairing that habit with cohort-level detail — rather than
just aggregate totals — is what turns a KPI dashboard into an early
warning system instead of a rear-view mirror.

If you're building this out for the first time, start simple: pick one
signup week, track it for eight weeks, and see what falls off and when.
